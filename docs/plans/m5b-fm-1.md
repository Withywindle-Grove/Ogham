# M5b · fm-1: a forced march in the party's log, with the player's Undo

**Ruled by Francis, 2026-11-10** (rewalk, sitting 1, check 23): "the forced march in the log, visible to the party and undoable by the player: yes", then, on how: **A**. 09 line: 2026-11-10, [expedition].

## What was wrong
A player's forced march changed the day for the whole group (+2 points, a rest due that evening) at one tap. The player who did it had no line for it in their own log, even in the verbose view, so nothing to undo; the GM alone saw "… forced the march with the party". Cause (the Builder's trace): an event's audience is one of `all`, `gm` or `owner`; travel events come from the travel group's row, which has no audience function, so they are the GM's.

## The plan (option A)
1. **A new audience, `group`:** an event may be addressed to the members of one travel group and the GM. The event carries its group; the read rule gains "I am in that group". The event core and its read rule change, so by `.claude/README.md` this step is built on **Fable, high** (Francis's "A" is that yes).
2. **Applied to the forced march and its Undo only**, for now. The other travel lines (the GM's move, the day's budget, the start of the day) stay the GM's until the ⚑ below is ruled.
3. **The player's Undo:** the player who marched can undo it from the toast and from the log line; the database already refuses with "that's changed since" once points are spent or the day has moved, and that refusal is shown with its reason. A permission case each way in `src/testing/permissionCases.ts`.
4. **The toast** at the moment of marching carries Undo (today it has none).
5. **A migration:** built and tested on the test project in its turn (the db-test trial), with the hidden-data batch; pushed to main by Francis only.

## Hidden means hidden (the check the builder stopped on)
In a split party, players of another group, or of no group, must not read this group's travel row (its budget, what is spent, the place being entered). The `group` audience keeps that. Tests, written first: a member of the group reads the line; a member of another group does not; a player in no group does not; the GM reads all; the event's stored row is not sent to anyone outside the group.

## Departures
(The builder keeps this section current in the pull request. Prepared 2026-11-10/11. Ruled by Francis, 2026-11-11 (`design/rulings/m5b-morning-rulings-2026-11-11.md`): item 2 as fm-2, item 1 as fm-3, item 7 as fm-4, item 3 as fm-5, and the marcher who has left the group (item 5's "still in that group") as fm-6. fm-1's migration is applied on TEST and main; #662's fix-forward (item 10) is applied nowhere yet.)

1. **Who reads a group's line: those in the group when it was written, not those in it now.** The plan's read rule was "I am in that group". Group numbers are used again by the next split (`20261030230000_m5_party_groups.sql`), so that rule would hand an earlier Group 2's travel to a later Group 2 made of other people, and a late joiner would read the group's earlier lines. The line now keeps the group's number and the players in it at that moment (`events.group_number`, `events.group_user_ids`); they and the GM read it. A player who leaves keeps the lines of their time. (Design's recommendation (b), 2026-11-10.)
2. **The place a group is entering is the GM's until it has been shown to that group, in the database** (⚑ 2, the database way; prepared 2026-11-11 on the coordinator's word, waiting for Francis's yes because it moves data in play). The #528 look-back found that `travel_move` copied the GM's own name for the place (typed, or `places.name` of a pack's place) into the travel row the group's players read whole. Now the GM's name and the place's id live in a GM-only side row (`travel_group_secrets`), and the row's `pending_place_name` holds only what that group's players may read: the place's players' name once one of its pieces has been shown to them, else nothing. The log's lines, the card ("Entering the next place, N more tomorrow") and the toasts follow from that; the words are in one place, `TRAVEL_PLACE_WORDS` in `src/core/travelWords.ts`. A line says what was known when it was written: a place shown later doesn't rename an earlier line. Also in it (the look-back's nit): a place undone before the group arrives is no place, and the group arrives somewhere unnamed.
3. **Only the five travel commands' lines are the group's** (the start of the day, Move…, the day's budget, Spend the day looking, the forced march) and their undos. Any other change to the travel row keeps the GM's line, as before: an activity's cost (the activity has its own line for everyone) and a secret activity the GM accepted ("players see nothing until the GM narrates"; with every change on the row addressed to the group, a group-mate would have read "answered the party: No" with the cost).
4. **The database did not refuse an Undo once the points were spent, when nothing was owed at the march** (the plan said it already did): the march's line holds only the total and the forced count, and a later Move… changes `spent`. Added the refusal ("That's changed since. Undo the later change first."), for the GM's undo too.
5. **The player's Undo needs the database's rights.** A player can't write the travel row (the GM's to update) nor the group's place an arrival moved, so `undo_request` sends a player's own forced march through `private.undo_own_march` (security definer, as `force_march` is): only the caller's own `travel.forceMarch` line, only while they are still in that group, only when the request wrote nothing outside a march's own tables (`travel_groups`, `travel_group_secrets`, `group_places`, `places`, `place_notes`). The checks and writes are the ordinary undo's.
6. **Notes on a group's line follow it** (the standing rule: a note takes its entry's audience): `notes.group_user_ids`, copied from the line; not in the plan, needed so a note on a travel line doesn't reach the other group.
7. **Old travel lines are not opened** to the groups: they stay the GM's (widening existing lines is a data change; see ⚑).
8. **The line's group comes from the line** (`group_number`), so a change's line says "Group 2" and not "the party" for a split group (the change's values never repeated the group; the GM's lines had the same slip).
9. **⚑ 2 (the words):** the existing sentences are kept ("You forced the march with the party: +4 points, rest due this evening."; another reader sees the marcher's display name, or "Someone" when there is none, never an email); the lead settles them.
10. **Next day's arrival reads the GM's part of the row** (fix, 2026-11-13, branch `fix-travel-day-arrival-secrets`; found when this change and the fix for F86/F87, `20261112100000_fix_travel_day_with_clock.sql`, met on the databases). Main's `private.start_travel_days` sent an arriving group to the travel row's own place name, which since item 2 is the players' name (or nothing): the group arrived at a place called by the players' name, or somewhere unnamed, and the GM's part kept the place. Not a leak. A fix-forward migration, `20261114100000_fix_travel_day_arrival_secrets.sql`, replaces that one function so it reads and clears the GM's part as `private.pay_owed` does; it rewrites no row (a group that already arrived wrongly stays where it is). In the app, `standsInTheWay` (ud-2) reads the entering place's name from the GM's part's line, and the stand-in database's `advance_clock` follows the migration. The order of commits departs from tests-first in one way: the merge of main could not pass the check with main's two tests failing, so the merge commit carries the least that makes them pass (the two reads above); the failing tests for the rest came next, then the fix.
