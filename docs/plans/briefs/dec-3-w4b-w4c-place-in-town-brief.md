`docs/design/rulings/dec-pack-town-players-2026-11-14.md`:

The lead's limits, which bind: a place **one level under the town of the group's current visit**; **exactly what a shown piece sends today**; **the place's own row stays the GM's**; **no person unless written into a shown piece**; **nothing after the group leaves**.

## The facts this is built on (the Builder's read, 2026-11-15)
- A place in town is today only an entry in the town's `places.town` (name, kind, shown, open), read by the GM alone. A town's own place is keyed `place:<entryId>` (T-b, #862), `entryId` being the pack entry id. It has no place row and no pieces until one is made. Place rows come from `make_place_from_pack` ("made, or found"); import makes none. `places`, `place_pieces` and `piece_shows` are read by the GM only; players get pieces through definer functions.
- A shown piece sends a player: group number, the place's players' name, the piece's id, kind, body and lead (`scene_pieces`). Never `gm_value`, `gm_trigger` or `gm_stat_line`; `scene_pieces` sends no `asset_id`. A picture is a piece of kind `picture`, read through `scene_pictures`, which sends its `asset_id` and at most one picture for a group; the stored image is readable through `private.place_picture_shown_to_me`.
- `scene_town` sends a player, for each **shown** entry of the town: group number, the town's players' name, `entry_key`, the entry's name, kind and open. It tests "a town" as `safety is not null or settlement is not null`.
- "The group is in that town now" is `private.town_visit` (the newest expedition, ended or not). `show_place_piece` uses `current_group_place`, which needs an expedition under way.
- `show_place_piece` refuses when: not the GM or no visit; the visit is undone or moved on, or the group's current place is not this visit's; the piece is not live or not of the visit's place. A repeat changes nothing.
- **The invariant every existing reader relies on:** a row of `piece_shows` belongs to a piece of its visit's own place. Eleven functions depend on it (`scene_pieces`, `scene_pictures`, `scene_town`, `known_places_campaign`, `known_places`, `private.visit_shown`, `back_at_place`, `private.follow_into_group`, `private.entering_name_for_players`, `private.place_name_for_me`, `campaign_ways_on`). **This work keeps it.**

## What must not change
`piece_shows`, `private.piece_show_rules`, `show_place_piece` and the eleven functions above: not one line. The only existing function this work touches is `private.place_picture_shown_to_me` (step 5), and the only things it adds to existing tables are two new triggers of its own: one on `place_pieces` (step 4, the tick) and one on the membership change (step 3, the reset marker for an emptied group). If the work seems to need a change to any other existing function, policy or trigger, stop and ask.

## What to build

### 1. One test, used throughout
A private function answering: **is this place one level under the town that this visit is in?** True only when all hold: the visit's place is a town, by the very test `scene_town` uses (`safety is not null or settlement is not null`); the place and the visit's place are in the same campaign and carry the same pack id; the place's stored parent is the visit's place (the place's `places.pack_parent_id`, a pack entry id held as text, equals the visit's place's `pack_entry_id`); neither place is undone; the visit is not undone. Two levels under is false. A place made by hand (no pack parent) is false. Not callable by clients. Every function below calls this one; none restates it.

**The place's entry in the town** is the entry of the town's `places.town` whose key equals `'place:' || pack_entry_id` of the place. Never match by name. No such entry means the place is not a place in that town: false.

### 2. The place's row
Made **lazily**, by the existing "made, or found" call, when the GM first opens an eye on something of it: not at import, not when the town is entered. Its pieces are made unshown, as today. No new way of making a place.

### 3. The store (new, add-only)
One new table for the shows of pieces of places in town: for a campaign, **the group's visit to the town** (the `group_places` row) and a piece, whether it is **shown**, and whether the row is undone; the group number copied from the visit; who, when and the request tag as `piece_shows` has them (you choose the table's and columns' names; mirror `piece_shows` where it has the same thing). One live row for a visit and a piece. It is a table of its own: **no row of it is ever in `piece_shows`.** Row-level security: **the campaign's GM alone** has policies on it (select, insert, update: the app's Undo runs as the caller and needs them, exactly as on `piece_shows`); **no player has any policy or reads it, ever**; no delete for anyone. Its own rules trigger (a copy of `private.piece_show_rules`' shape) refuses, whoever writes: a row whose piece is undone at insert; a visit or piece not of the same campaign; a row for which test 1 is false; any later change other than shown, its time and undone; **and, at insert with shown true and at any change that turns shown from false to true or brings back an undone row whose shown is true, a visit that is undone, moved on, or not the group's current place by `current_group_place`** (so the expedition-under-way requirement of ge-1's "every other check" holds for a direct write too, and a GM's write can never make readable what the function in step 4 would refuse). One consequence, to raise as a ⚑ and not to work round: Undo of a hide is refused once the expedition has ended. Nothing is deleted: hiding sets shown to false. Wire it for Undo and the log as `piece_shows` is (its entry among the undoable tables and makings; its `public.logged_commands` row, verbose and the GM's alone; no audience function, so its lines are the GM's).

**A row must not outlive the people it was shown to** (the worker's finding, 2026-11-15). A visit's group number cannot change, but a group that empties (its last member leaves in a join-up or a split) keeps its visit live, and a later split into that number re-uses the old visit for other players (`private.follow_into_group` keeps the old row when the place and `private.shown_pieces` match, and `shown_pieces` knows nothing of the store). So: **when a group empties, its place-in-town shows are reset, by a marker and not by changing any store row** (a logged change made inside a player's split would become part of that player's action, and their own Undo would then fail on a row they cannot read; and no trigger may write the session's request settings to avoid that):
- **A reset marker.** A second small table of the store's own: a campaign, a group number and a time, "this group number's place-in-town shows were reset at T" (by group number and not by visit, so that it covers every visit of that number, moved on or not: an Undo of a move can make an old visit current again). GM-only policies for reading, none for players, no log trigger and no Undo wiring: it is derived state, not an action (the membership change that caused it is already logged), and nothing is erased by it.
- **One new trigger of the store's own** on `party_group_members` (after an update of the group number, the event `private.follow_into_group` listens to, with that function's own emptiness test) inserts a marker, at the database's own time, **for a group number that has just emptied and for a group number that has just been filled from empty** (both, so that a number emptied by a path the trigger does not see is still reset when someone next comes into it). It fires after an insert, after an update of the group number and after a delete of a member's row, is security definer like `follow_into_group`, and takes a row lock on that group number's visits before it writes, as the show function in step 4 locks its visit, so a show and a reset cannot cross. It updates no store row. **Not covered, raise as a ⚑:** a character already in an otherwise empty group who becomes active again by a change of status or approval alone (no member row changes); `follow_into_group` has the same blind spot today. `follow_into_group`, `private.shown_pieces`, `private.log_change`, the Undo functions and `group_places`' rules are **not edited**.
- **"Shown now"** is one private function, used by every reader and by the command: the store's row is live, shown is true, **and its shown time is later than the latest reset marker of its campaign and group number**, if there is one (a reset at the same instant as a show counts as after it). **The shown time is the database's, never the writer's:** the store's rules trigger sets it to now at an insert with shown true and whenever shown turns true, and treats any other attempt to change the shown time as turning shown on (the same guard as in the paragraph above applies, and the time is set to now), so no write, the GM's direct one included, can date a show. The one exception is the app's Undo (its reverting mark), which may only put the time back to an earlier value, never forward. The GM's own read of the eyes is a GM-only function (security invoker, so row-level security gives a player nothing) returning the piece ids that are "shown now" for a visit. The player reads in step 5 use it as test 3; the GM's read of the eyes uses it too, so the GM sees those eyes closed after a reset; the command in step 4 treats "shown, but before the reset" as not shown, so showing it again sets its shown time to now, an ordinary logged show.
- The GM opens the eyes again for whoever is in that group next; Undo of the join-up does not bring them back (raise it as a ⚑). An Undo of a move that makes an earlier visit current again brings back that visit's rows only where they are still "shown now": rows shown before the group number was last reset stay closed, whichever visit they are on. Test exactly that: A shown; the group moves to B; the group empties; another player fills the number; the GM undoes the move; the player reads nothing of A's places.

### 4. W4c: the GM's eye (ge-1)
A **new** command and function, "show (or hide) a piece of a place in town", with `can(user, command, target)` and an event, logged like `show_place_piece` (its row in `public.logged_commands`; the line is the GM's alone). It restates **every** check of `show_place_piece`, changing only the one ge-1 allows:
- the GM of the campaign only;
- the group's visit exists, is not undone and not moved on, and is the group's current place by `current_group_place` (**the expedition-under-way requirement stays**, as for a town's own pieces);
- the piece is live;
- **the piece's place is one level under that visit's place** (test 1) where `show_place_piece` requires it to be the visit's place itself;
- the visit and the piece are of the same campaign;
- a repeat changes nothing; hiding a piece never shown returns nothing; a repeated request (the request tag) is answered once, as `show_place_piece` does.

The row is written on **that visit**. Undo puts the row back as it was. Players' screens are told to refresh by a copy of the tick a show uses today (`tick_piece_show`: a number only, read by the GM and the group, revealing nothing). When a piece of a place in town is undone, its groups must be ticked too: `tick_piece` finds groups through `piece_shows` alone, so add **one new trigger** on `place_pieces` that ticks the groups found in the store; `tick_piece` itself is not edited. That added trigger is the one thing this work adds to an existing table.

### 5. W4b: what a player reads (tr-1)
Two **new** definer functions (pieces; pictures). A player receives a piece of a place in town only when **all** hold:
1. the player is in the group (`private.in_my_group`) of the one visit that `private.town_visit` returns for that campaign and group: the same test `scene_town` uses. **The lead's reading of "while their group is in that town"** (told to Francis): it holds between expeditions while the visit is not moved on, exactly as the town's own list does today, and stops when the visit is moved on or undone;
2. test 1 is true for the piece's place against that visit;
3. the piece is not undone and it is **"shown now"** on that same visit (step 3's one function: a live row with shown true, later than the latest reset of its campaign and group number);
4. **the place's own entry in the town is shown at the time of the read** (the lead's, narrower than tr-1: a part of a place the players have not been shown never arrives ahead of the place; when the GM hides the entry, its parts stop arriving at once, and the store's rows stay as they are);
5. **the piece is not of kind `person`** (the lead's, narrower than tr-1, until W8: a person's piece made from the pack holds every line, and np-3 gives players only the revealed ones; W8's pp-1 lifts this for a body that holds only revealed lines);
6. **the town itself is shown to the group on that visit**, by the very condition `scene_town` applies before it sends anything (a piece of the town's own shown on the visit; call the same private test, do not restate it; the lead's, narrower than tr-1: a part of a place never reaches a player before the town does).

Wherever this brief says "tests 1, 2, 3 and 4" for a picture or its stored image, test 6 holds as well.

**Pieces: what is sent, and nothing else.** Exactly `scene_pieces`' columns (group number; the place's name as `coalesce(players_name, name)`, never the GM's name where a players' name exists; piece id; kind; body and lead through `private.plain_link_text`), plus that entry's `entry_key` exactly as `scene_town` already sends it to that player. Never: the place's row or its id; any `gm_*` field; a count of unshown pieces; the pack's entry or its id.

**Pictures.** For each place that passes tests 1, 2, 4 and 6, at most its one picture piece that passes test 3: `entry_key` and the fields `scene_pictures` sends for a picture (its `asset_id` among them), and nothing else. A place in town's picture never replaces the town's own, which `scene_pictures` goes on sending unchanged.

**The stored picture (tr-1's word "picture").** `private.place_picture_shown_to_me` keeps its present branch unchanged and gains one more: the asset asked for is the `asset_id` of a live piece of kind `picture` of a place in town that passes tests 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 **for the player asking** (the signed-in user, through `private.in_my_group`). That branch calls the same private test the picture function uses; neither restates it. It lets a player download exactly the picture that function returns to them and no other asset. Francis's yes for it is tr-1 ("…description, parts, **picture** and keeper's lines…"); it is named to him before the main push.

**When the group leaves and comes back.** With the visit moved on, both functions and the storage branch return nothing of that town's places. **A new visit starts with nothing shown of the town's places** (the lead's, the narrow reading of "nothing after the group leaves"): back in the town, or as a new group after a split, the GM opens the eyes again. Nothing is copied from an earlier visit; `back_at_place` and `private.follow_into_group` are not touched. Whether a return should find them still shown, as a town's own pieces are today, is a question for Francis: raise it as a ⚑, do not build it.

## Tests (database, and the stand-in the same)
- A player in the town reads a shown piece of a shown place one level under it: the columns above and no others (assert the full key list of each row).
- The same player reads nothing when: the piece is not shown; the piece is undone; the place's entry is not shown; the place is two levels under; the place belongs to another town; the place was made by hand; the visit is moved on; the visit is undone. The entry hidden after a piece was shown: the piece stops arriving; shown again: it arrives again.
- A piece of kind `person` is never read by a player, shown or not.
- Nothing of a place in town arrives while no piece of the town itself is shown on the visit; it arrives once one is.
- A group number reused after a join-up reads nothing an earlier group of that number was shown: group 2 is shown a piece of a place in town; its last member joins group 1; another player later splits into group 2 in the same town; that player reads nothing of the place until the GM shows it again, and the GM's read says not shown meanwhile. A player joining a group that still has members reads what that group is shown. **A player's own Undo of a split that emptied a group with a place-in-town show still works** (the reset writes no logged line). No player reads the marker table.
- Undo of a show and of a hide works for the GM; a piece of a place in town undone ticks its groups; a player can neither read nor write the store.
- A player of another group, a member with no character in the group, an outsider and another campaign read nothing; none of them, and no player, reads the store's table directly.
- The GM's name of a place with a players' name appears nowhere in a player's payload.
- A picture: read under its tests with the entry's key; not read when any fails; the town's own picture from `scene_pictures` is unchanged beside it. Storage: a player in the town downloads the shown picture of a shown place in town; cannot when the piece is hidden, the entry is hidden, the visit is moved on, or they are in another group, another campaign or outside; no other asset of the place becomes readable.
- Between expeditions (the expedition ended, the visit not moved on) a player still reads what was shown. After moving on: nothing. Back in the town on a new visit: nothing until the GM shows it again; the earlier visit's rows are still there.
- **The existing readers are untouched in effect:** with a piece of a place in town shown, each of `scene_pieces`, `scene_pictures`, `scene_town`, `known_places_campaign`, `known_places` and `campaign_ways_on` returns exactly what it returned before; a move away and back, and a split, succeed.
