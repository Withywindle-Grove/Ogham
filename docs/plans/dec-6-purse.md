## What it is
Each character has a purse: coin by denomination, as the campaign's setting names its coins, in integers. Only its owner and the GM read it. The party stash holds coin too, which every player of the party reads.

| Who | May |
|---|---|
| The GM | Give coin to a character's purse or the stash; take coin from either; read every purse |
| A player | Read their own characters' purses and the stash's coin; spend from their own purse (it leaves play, with an optional few words on what for); give from their own purse to another character or to the stash; take coin from the stash |
| Other players | Nothing of a living character's purse. A Down or dead character's purse opens to them to take from, every take logged, closing again if the character recovers (ruled 2026-10-29; the purse half of that rule becomes real here) |

Every change is one logged command with Undo, refused with its reason once the coin has moved on ("That's changed since. Undo the later change first.").

## Boundaries (the rest of M7b, not here)
- Coin weighs nothing in the load.
- Buying charges nothing by itself: the GM's In stock adds the item, and the price is taken off the purse by hand (the player's Spend, or the GM's take).
- No change-making between denominations, no shares, no party fund beyond the stash's coin.
- The log's audience for a purse change is the owner and the GM; for the stash's coin, the party. A line never shows an amount to someone who can't read that purse.
- The migration is built and held: no TEST turn and no push without Francis's word through the lead.

### Piece (a), the data and its read rule (branch `dec-6a-purse-data`, 2026-11-11)
1. **The migration is stamped `20261111160000`**, not 150000: #637 took that stamp (the lead's word, the same day).
2. **The commands' `logged_commands` rows and the Undo list's row are left to piece (b).** The health test (`supabase/tests/m3-health.test.ts`) fails on a logged command the app doesn't have, so the rows must arrive with the commands. Undo runs with the caller's own rights, so it needs the write rules that piece (b) brings; a row in `undoable_tables` now would be untested. What (a) does carry of the log: purse changes are logged by the ordinary log trigger, and who sees those lines is decided (the owner and the GM; everyone for the stash).
3. **Nobody writes a purse from a device in (a)**: read rule only, no insert, update or delete rule and no grant. Piece (b) needs its own small migration (the commands' functions, the two lists' rows), or adds to this file while it is still applied nowhere: the lead's call.
4. **Down is worked out, not stored.** The plan says "the Down-or-dead exception as the inventory has it", but the inventory has none in the database: every member already reads an approved character's pack, and there is no stored Down state before M6. The purse rule reads Down as the app shows it today: an approved character in play (active or benched) with HP at or under the setting's `death.at` (the top rules set that says it, optional rules applied); dead is the character's status. With no `death.at`, only dead opens a purse. One function (`private.is_down_or_dead`) holds the test, for M6 to replace.
5. **The GM does not read the purse of another person's draft**, as with the draft's gear (ruled 2026-10-26). The plan's table says the GM reads every purse; a draft is the one exception, and it is empty until creation pays into it.
6. **A party's or the GM's own hireling's purse is the GM's alone** (nobody owns it); its pack stays readable by all, as today.

### Piece (b), the commands (branch `dec-6-b-purse-commands`, 2026-11-13)
1. **Its own migration, `20261114090000_dec_6b_purse_commands.sql`** (piece a's is merged, so it can't be added to). Stamped 20261114090000, after 20261113230000 (the newest on main or in an open pull request on 2026-11-13; renamed from 160000 before it was applied anywhere).
2. **Five commands, not four:** `purse.add` and `purse.remove` (the GM), `purse.spend`, `purse.give` and `purse.take` (a player). The GM also may run `purse.take`, moving coin from any purse into any approved character's; the GM does **not** run `purse.spend` (the GM's own take is `purse.remove`). Nobody gives to, or takes into, a draft or a waiting character; the GM gives to and takes from every purse but another person's draft (piece a's Departure 5).
3. **A player's Undo of their own coin line runs as the database.** A give reaches a purse the giver can't read, and its log line is the receiver's; Undo as the player would see only half the action and put back only the giver's side. So `public.undo_request` (replaced from its newest definition, `20261021160000_m2_undo_as_caller.sql`) sends a player's own `purse.spend`, `purse.give` or `purse.take` line through `private.undo_coin_as_author`, as the GM's undo has always run: only the player's own line that they can see, with its request id, and only an action that is exactly what the purse functions make (every line on a purse and of the picked line's type; a spend its one line; a give's or take's lines balancing coin by coin), so Undo never makes coin (review, 2026-11-13). **A take goes back only while its player may still read the purse it came from** (the stash; a character still Down or dead); after the character recovers it is refused with "That's changed since. Undo the later change first." and the GM moves the coin back (the lead, 2026-11-13): Undo never writes into a purse its player can't read, except the receiving side of their own give (pu-9). The usual checks hold. This changes who may **write** (undo) a purse, not who may read one.
3a. **Every purse function names its own lines and needs the request's id** (review, 2026-11-13): it sets `grimoire.event_type` itself, never trusting the device's header, and refuses a request without its id ("That change needs its request id. Try again from the app."). It checks who is asking on an unlocked read and locks the purses only once allowed. A repeat on either purse of a move is a repeat of the whole move.
3b. **Every coin Undo, the GM's too, is held to one check** (the Fable review and the lead, 2026-11-13): `private.coin_action_lines` refuses an action with any reversal line in it, or not exactly as the functions make it; for a player it also reads the purses as they are now (their side still theirs and in play, a give's receiver still open to coin, a take's purse still theirs and its source still open to them), refusing with "That's changed since. Undo the later change first." `private.undo_coin_checked` locks the action's purses in one order, files the reversal lines under the database's own request id (`private.log_change` reads `grimoire.request_id`), and afterwards requires that exactly the checked lines were reversed. `private.undo_as_gm` is replaced (from `20261021160000_m2_undo_as_caller.sql`) so the GM's Undo of any action touching a purse goes the same way; the rest of the GM's Undo is untouched. A purse function's own words (a spend's, a move's "with:…") now win over a GM ruling's reason.
3d. **A give or take is undone only as exactly two lines on two purses** (the Fable re-read, 2026-11-13): two moves under one request id are refused, because Undo puts each purse back to its oldest line's value and would wipe another action's change in between. A purse function now locks its purses before its repeat check, so a retry arriving while its original is still at work waits and then reads as a repeat. A retry whose own transaction began first can still apply twice, a window of milliseconds; the doubled action is then refused to Undo by the two-line rule, and the GM can move the coin back.
3g. **A second migration, `20261114093000_dec_6b_keep_fm1_in_core.sql`** (2026-11-13), fixes forward on the first, which is applied on TEST: the first replaced `private.log_change` and `public.undo_request` from their newest bodies on main, missing fm-1's newer ones (`20261111100000_fm1_group_log_audience.sql`, #628, unmerged but applied on TEST and main: a travel group's log audience, a player's own Undo of a forced march). The fix restores fm-1's bodies with the purse additions merged in. **#628 must merge before this pull request**: the fix's bodies use fm-1's columns and functions.
3f. **Only an approved character's own purse is its player's to spend or give from** (the Fable check, 2026-11-13): never a draft's or a waiting character's, though the player reads it (piece a). It protects a later "starting coin" piece. And a player's Undo of a take from their own Down or dead character or hireling is refused once the GM has archived it (the source must still be open to coin).
3e. **For later:** the GM's Undo refuses any action with a purse line and another table's line together. Nothing makes one today; a future command that does both in one request (a "buy" taking coin and adding the item, say) needs its own Undo rule first.
3c. **An Undo of coin can't itself be undone, by anyone** (the lead, 2026-11-13). The GM reads an ordinary refusal: "An Undo of coin can't be undone. Move the coin again instead." Nothing changes. Why: less SQL in the core (no redo path to check); the GM can move the coin again with the purse commands; and the log keeps both lines, the change and its Undo.
4. **What a spend was for is kept as the log line's reason.** `private.log_change` (replaced from its newest definition, `20261030161000_m5_light.sql`) also reads `grimoire.event_reason`, which only the purse functions set; a purse function's own words win over a GM ruling's reason (3b). Before this, only a GM ruling had a reason. A give's or a take's two lines keep the other side there instead ("with:<character id>" or "with:stash", `private.coin_counterpart`), so each side's line names both characters (pu-7) though neither reader can read the other purse; never an amount.
5. **A refusal for too little coin comes from the database** (code GR422, with the purse's name, the coin and what it holds as detail); the app turns it into the approved words, "<Name> has 30 herons.", with the setting's coin name when the screen passes the names in. The stash reads "The stash has 0 herons." (the lead, 2026-11-13). A bad amount reads "Say how many: a whole number, 1 or more." (the lead, 2026-11-13).
6. **A coin that reaches zero leaves the purse's map** (a coin that isn't there counts 0, as piece a has it). **A purse holds at most 999,999,999 of a coin** (piece a's cap, kept): past it, "<Name> can't hold more than 999,999,999 herons." (code GR423; the lead's words, 2026-11-13) where the one acting can read that purse (their own, the stash, the GM). To a giver who can't read the receiver's purse, the refusal names no one: "That give didn't go through. Nothing changed." (code GR424; the lead, 2026-11-13).
7. **The log lines are built in `src/core/events.ts`, in the approved words** (Francis, 2026-11-13: "Piece c's pictures and words" above, and pu-7; the scope check is closed): "<Name> spent 2 herons: <what for>" ("<Name> spent 2 herons" with nothing said); "Your GM gave <Name> 40 herons"; "Your GM took 10 herons from <Name>"; "<Name> gave <Other> 5 herons" (the one line both read); "<Name> put 10 herons in the stash"; "<Name> took 10 herons from the stash"; "Your GM put 20 herons in the stash"; "Your GM took 10 herons from the stash"; "<Name> took 10 herons from <Other>" (only a player's take from a Down or dead character). Characters' names, never players'; no closing full stop, as written. **The GM's moves are never credited to a character** (the lead, 2026-11-13): "Your GM moved 10 herons from Wren to Moss." (one line, read by both players and the GM) and "Your GM gave Moss 10 herons from the stash." (the lead's words, with their full stop); the log's names now say who the GMs are. The log's names now load the purses the reader may read. The coin is in the setting's words through the coin-words helpers (#687): the log screen (`src/app/LogView.tsx`) loads the setting and passes `coinAmount` into the log's names, since core neither reads the setting nor imports a module; a coin the setting doesn't list shows by its key.
8. **An archived character's purse follows its pack** (the lead, 2026-11-13): only the GM changes it (gives to it, takes from it, or moves its coin to another character); its player can't spend, give or take from it, nor can anyone but the GM give or take into it (refused in the database and in `allowed`).
