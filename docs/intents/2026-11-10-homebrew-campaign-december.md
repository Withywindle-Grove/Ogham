# Intent: the homebrew campaign that starts on 5 December

Author: Francis · Date: 2026-11-10 · Status: draft (written down by the design agent from his words, during the M5b gate rewalk; not yet discussed)

## Problem
Francis is building a homebrew campaign that kicks off on **5 December 2026**, "with as much functionality as the app allows at that time". It runs on classic-advanced's rules but is set, at its foundation, in the fae-woodland region. Today the region pack is written for fae-woodland's rules, the conversion pack covers the setting's classes, kindreds, items and shared monsters but not the region, and the app has no hex map of a world wider than one region.

## Outcome
In his words: "The campaign will be [a classic-advanced] rules campaign, but will be set with a foundation in [the fae-woodland] region, and therefore that part of the world needs conversion as does the rest of the pack. We'll need a hex-based world map again, using [the fae-woodland region] as the centre of that map, with a nearby coast and port town to be described later. Players will travel from [a named settlement in the region's upland; the name is kept out of the repo] to a ship in the port town and take that ship to an island-based adventure."

Added the same evening: "Pieces that are completely homebrew include the map outside of [the fae-woodland region] and the port town." (Whether the island adventure is homebrew or from a book: not said.)

So at the table on 5 December: classic-advanced characters start in a settlement of the converted region, cross it by hex to a port on a nearby coast, and sail to an island where the adventure is.

**Scope narrowed (Francis, 2026-11-10, evening):** "We'll narrow what we need Dec 5 to the port city and the adventure. Hex map comes later, and we'll limit the [fae-woodland] conversion to some item and shop tables, and kindreds and classes (details to be discussed)." So for 5 December: the port town and the island adventure. Not for 5 December: the hex world map and travel across it; the region's full conversion. The conversion is limited to some item and shop tables, the kindreds and the classes.

**Also that evening:** "Play starts at port. For now [fae-woodland] kindred can only play [fae-woodland] classes and vice versa." So the app's campaign begins in the port town (the road from the region is not played), and the two settings' peoples and classes don't mix: a fae-woodland kindred takes only a fae-woodland class, and a classic-advanced race takes only a classic-advanced class.

**On travel:** "Only travel covered in the adventure is needed (largely point to point)." So the travel the app needs for 5 December is the adventure's own: moving between the harbour's linked spots by turns (rowing, swimming), the sea leg between the port and the island, and movement between and inside the island's sites; no hex travel.

**The port, as he described it that evening (names and details are in the private pack notes; twenty-one rulings there):** the port town is the island adventure's own port city, one of the seven cities of its sea. "I imagine the town to be ruled by a corrupt and bureaucratic church, almost like Rome and Venice matched. Maybe a 'papal states region'." "Even though it's a church town, it's still seedy, and there's a vibrant and sometimes murky common folk and adventuring tradition (it is a port town): that's where [his own earlier town]'s influence is imbued. Instead of going down the stairs, adventuring parties usually 'go on a march' or 'board a ship' or 'accept a contract', so there's still a fatalistic and mercenary aspect to that part of the town." Its church is a sister communion of the region's faith, to the west of the region on his map: "akin to the difference between Catholic / Eastern Orthodox", rich, sensual and mystical; its icons and mosaics show "strange monsters, demons and spirits … all aspects of the central figure, who is 'ineffable' … and infuses all", and that figure is never shown. It regards the other communions as cousins: "wrong, but family". The region's church is not recharacterised: "The [fae-woodland] source material is of exceptional quality narratively. Keep to its spirit as closely as you can." The classic-advanced cleric picks a patron (thirteen to start: the adventure's own horror is one, its priests are present in the city, and everything in the adventure's appendix on places and people is carried into the port's design); the fae-woodland cleric and friar stay as their book has them. Wanted in the town: the Wall of Names, the two inns of his earlier town plus a fancy inn and a tavern or two, room for other cults and faiths beside the church.

## Touches
- **Packs (private):** the region pack converted to classic-advanced's terms (fae-woodland-region through conv-fae-woodland-to-classic, or a converted region pack: to decide); the rest of the setting pack converted, as today's conversion does for classes, kindreds, items and monsters; a homebrew pack for the wider world: the coast, the port town, the island (to be described by Francis later).
- **App:** a hex-based world map with the region at its centre and land beyond it (M8b, the hex map and point-to-point travel design session; the party map from the players' feedback); travel across it under classic-advanced's travel rules; a sea crossing (vehicles and water travel: what the app models today is to check); an island adventure (adventure packs, M8).
- **Design sessions owed that this feeds:** M8b and the party map; the conversion question of 2026-11-10 (fae-woodland's classes and kindreds in a classic-advanced campaign; which races may take which classes); conversion packs both ways (08, parked).

## Constraints
- The date is fixed; the scope is "as much as the app allows at that time". Whatever isn't built is run by hand, and the plan should say which.
- IP rules: private packs stay private; the repo uses the aliases and invented examples only.
- Hidden means hidden for the map: what the players haven't found stays unsent.

## Out of scope
Not said yet. The port town and the island are "to be described later".
