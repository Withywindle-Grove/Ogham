# The linked-spots picks, and how a stat block layout's names match (2026-11-13)

## Five picks on the place of linked spots
The Design Studio's picks on the plan's open list, put to Francis as one question. His word: **"Approve the five"**.

| Id | Ruling |
|---|---|
| dp-1 | On a map, a touch that lifts within about 10 px and half a second is a tap; anything longer or further is a drag. |
| dp-2 | A fact's value that can't fit one line, after its wording and size have been tried, is cut with an ellipsis and shows whole on a tap. |
| dp-3 | An empty map frame has no tools under it until there is a map. |
| dp-4 | A proposed way that arrives while the GM has another sheet open joins the queue: the open sheet is not displaced and the count updates (as mc-42a's first in, first out). |
| dp-5 | After the GM answers No at an obstacle, players see only the obstacle's name on that way's row: never the book's note, the extra time or the chance. Those never reach a player's device (hard rule 5). |

The plan's other three open items were already settled by his earlier rulings: a rule's icon comes from the app's set named by the pack (ds-6, mc-49a, mc-52); no glyph where a roll names no die (ds-3); the map's title at the left of its tools where it adds something (mc-44a).

## A stat block layout's names (the lead's, technical)
| Id | Ruling |
|---|---|
| pf-13b | A layout's field name matches a creature's field **exactly, case included**. `hp`, in lower case only, is the reserved name for hit points with their average; a field a pack spells "HP" is an ordinary field. Hiding one never hides the other. |

Why: one shipped pack hides a field spelled in capitals on a single creature, because it repeats that creature's hit points. A reader that folded case would have hidden the hit points of every creature in the pack. The Pack Creator caught it before any reader had merged; the reader is made exact, with a test, before it does; the checker already matched exactly.
