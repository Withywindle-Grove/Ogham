# Downtime (design ruling, 2026-11-02)

The downtime design session before M7 (08, ST14). A survey of the sources first (`design/notes/downtime-survey-2026-11-02.md`), then a paper test with Francis: eight invented characters in a market town, each with one significant action (training, founding a mercenary company, copying a scroll, working out and brewing a potion, winning over a prioress, carousing, ordering a contraption from a maker in another town, building a watchtower), a hireling, weekly costs, troubles with deadlines, and a second downtime. Walk: `design/notes/downtime-dry-run-notes.md`. Mockups: `design/mockups/downtime/01–07` (pictures in `downtime/png/`). Ids `dt-1` … `dt-45`. All approved by Francis on 2026-11-02.

**The spirit:** downtime is narrative first. The player says what the character does and how; the GM sets the shape; the app keeps the memory (what's due, what's running, what went wrong and by when) so nothing is forgotten between sessions, and keeps the screens visual rather than walls of text.

## A. Getting into downtime
- **dt-2 Two taps.** End expedition and Start downtime stay separate: the party may set out again at once.
- **dt-3 The loot split and Award XP belong to End expedition**, not to Start downtime. (Award XP stays available any time, as 05 says.)
- **dt-9 The arrival card.** Arriving at a **Safe** settlement, by **Where?** or by **Move…**, puts a quiet card on the GM's console by itself: "The party is back in a safe place" · **End expedition** (split, award, offer a full rest) · **Not now**. After End expedition the card offers **Start downtime** · **Not yet**. A Hostile settlement shows no card. Players see nothing. Replaces ST2's **Back in safety** / **Into town** buttons, so the ⚑ "who taps them" is gone: the GM, through the card.

## B. Town life
- **dt-8 Stored places are one thing.** Bank, vault, strongbox, cache: a stored place with a kind. The town's come from the pack or the GM; **+ Place** adds a private one.
- **dt-11 Lodging.** Players choose from the town's lodgings with their rates; only **Somewhere else…** needs the GM's yes. A stay can be **shared**: the player names who else stays, and it appears on their sheets too (up to a whole party in a rented cottage). A stay is a repeating spend under Due.
- **dt-12 A vault holds gear too,** with a fee from the pack or the GM. A rented room can include storage (free or in its price; the lodging can say so).
- **dt-13 Fees.** A fee in the pack goes on Due by itself. The GM can charge a fee at any time to the party fund or to any player (logged, Undo).
- **dt-14 Paying for a shared stay.** The booker picks: pay it all; pay from the party stash (if they're the Treasurer); send it to the Treasurer; or ask the others to chip in until it's paid (with **Split evenly**). With no Treasurer, any player may pay from the stash. *Complex: build it, simplify later if needed.*
- **dt-15 Spend…** on the purse: an amount and a short note ("tipped the barkeep"), no approval, no game effect, one log line with Undo. Everyone sees the line; **Hide** limits it to the player and the GM. Works on an adventure too.
- **dt-28 An unpaid Due** can be covered by: borrowing from another player; a bank loan (where the town has one); selling gear (buying and selling work any time in downtime); credit (the GM's yes); or another way the player and GM agree, which the GM types in.
- **dt-29 Paying by itself.** A player can set a cost to pay itself; they're asked only when the purse is short.
- **dt-30 The party treasury** can pay a cost by itself or settle any Due; with a Treasurer, the Treasurer approves.
- **dt-39 Costs keep running during expeditions.**

## C. Actions
- **dt-1 One significant action per character per downtime,** plus any number of small upkeep actions (paying, buying, running a business).
- **dt-23 Helping uses the helper's action.** Each action slot has **Pass** with a note in the player's words ("helped Garrick"); Pass also covers a quiet downtime.
- **dt-16 A new activity.** The player picks a pack activity or **Other** and says what they want and how, then sends it to the GM. A pack activity brings its fields; Other brings plain ones (Duration, Costs, Roll, Progress, At the end). The GM can **Ask a question** first (or talk at the table), then fills the terms and **Sends** them. The player **Approves** or **Sends back with a note**.
- **dt-18 Ongoing activities come first** in the player's list (Carry on), before anything new.
- **dt-17 Advancing.** An ongoing activity advances as its rules say. With no rule, the player taps **Advance** and the GM marks the progress and effects. When it advances by a roll, Advance asks for the roll.
- **dt-27 Time or a gate.** Activities measured in time move on by themselves. Activities with a gate (a roll, money) wait for the player: **Advance** or **Pause**. Pause costs nothing and rolls nothing.
- **dt-19 Progress steps are in R1** (moved from R2).
- **dt-20 The player rolls.**
- **dt-32 Tap the band.** When the result is a table the player can see, they roll and tap the band their total lands in; no number to type.
- **dt-34 The result applies by itself** within the rules and goes to the GM to approve and add the story.
- **dt-21 Bonuses are asked for with a reason** (spending more, help, going slowly, a favour, standing, a good scene); the GM grants them in the terms.
- **dt-22 The GM's note** on an activity is hidden by default, or shown to the player.
- **dt-37 Results of running something:** a fixed result (10 gp a week), the GM's words, a table the player rolls on (the pack's or the GM's homebrew), or another result the rules set.
- **dt-38 Upkeep that can't be skipped** (running the company): **Skip** counts as the worst result; the way out is **Disband…**.
- **dt-41 Upkeep comes round once per period,** set when the activity is agreed and changeable by the GM.
- **dt-40 Deliveries** land where the player said when ordering.

## D. Time and trouble
- **dt-24 Any amount of time.** The GM moves downtime by a day, a week, a month, any number of them, or a date; everything due multiplies (two weeks = two weeks of lodging), shown before the move and summed after.
- **dt-25 The players' summary.** After a jump each player gets a one-time card on the Scene (what happened, what was paid, what's due); Close dismisses it, the log keeps it.
- **dt-31 Deadlines** count in **downtimes** (the party's stretches between expeditions) or in **game time**. A trouble carries its text, its clock and what happens when the clock runs out; then the GM gets **Apply it · Give more time · Clear it**.
- **dt-35 Settling a trouble** is upkeep or a significant action, as the trouble (or the GM) says.
- **dt-36 Trouble is private** to the character's player and the GM; the player shares it in their own words at the table.

## E. Notes, information and items
- **dt-4 Identifying in play** uses the GM's existing **Reveal** (05 Unidentified items) at any moment.
- **dt-5 Two sheets:** the **party sheet** and each **player's own sheet** ("Notes and information"). Every player adds to the party sheet. Each note's writer and session are one tap away. The GM **Sends** rumours and information to either sheet, by hand or from a pack's table, with an optional GM-only note.
- **dt-6 No separate GM list:** the GM reads the sheets and the packs.
- **dt-7 The pack's Unidentified section** holds unknown items of every type; once fully revealed an item moves to its own section.
- **dt-10 Editing a party note:** its writer, the **Archivist** and the GM can edit or remove it (Undo).
- **dt-42 Kinds** (rumour, information, note) are available to the GM and to players.
- **dt-43 Share with the party** moves a note from a player's own sheet to the party sheet.
- **dt-44 Where they live:** the party sheet is a section of the **Company** page, with a "Notes · N" shortcut on the Scene; a player's own sheet is a sub-tab of the Player screen. With companies switched off, the party sheet sits on the Scene.
- **dt-45 One party sheet per company.**

## F. Look
- **dt-26 Progress bars,** segmented and built like the HP bar, always with words beside them. **Design rule (06):** screens stay visually interesting; prefer bars, badges and cards to lines of text.
- **dt-33 Under each bar:** the current step and the next one, with enough detail to act on.

## Proposed for 07 (⚑, not ruled)
A `downtime` section a rules pack, an overlay or a homebrew pack may carry (so a downtime supplement converts as an overlay on any setting):
- `activities[]`: `id`, `name`, `blurb`, `rules` (own words), `kind` (`significant` | `upkeep`), `skippable` (upkeep only; default true), `period` (upkeep), `duration` (text or `{ amount, unit }` with `unit` day · week · month · downtime), `costs[]` (`{ amount, per?, from? }`), `roll` (`{ dice, modifier?, bands[] }`, each band `{ range, label, result, progress? }`), `progress` (`{ steps[]: { name, detail } }`), `troubles` (a table id), `atTheEnd` (text), `fieldsForOther` (none; Other uses the plain fields).
- `troubles` are ordinary tables whose rows may add `clock` (`{ amount, unit }`) and `whenItRunsOut` (text, optional `progress: -1`).
- `lodgings[]` (`name`, `rate`, `per`, `sleeps?`, `storage?`) and `storedPlaces[]` (`name`, `kind`, `fee?`, `holdsItems?`) on settlements, folding the M5b settlement ⚑ on field names into this one.
- `gmPanels` for the supplement's worldbuilding prompts (already in the format).
