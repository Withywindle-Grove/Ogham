# Brief for the Design Studio: the Scene tab, redesigned (2026-11-10)

Written by the lead (the Designer session) for a second design session, as the trial ruled on 2026-11-10 (`docs/plans/after-walk-24-hours-2026-11-10.md`). **You produce mockups and a proposal note; you report to the lead ("Designer"); you never ask Francis for a ruling and never edit rulings, 09 or specs.** Start from this file and the files it names, not from history.

## Objective
Francis walked the Scene tab on the live app during the M5b gate rewalk (sitting 1, steps 7 to 10) and ruled on its look and arrangement. His verdict, in his words: "Mechanically everything seems to work (mostly). Visually, it's confusing and busy and needs optimizing. The two goals here are to automate busywork processes, procedures, and note-taking, and to quickly convey reference material to the GM and players. I'd say it does neither well." And: "pretty much redesign visually everything in Scene tab", "as always show me mockups first", "this whole page needs a style refresh, top to bottom".

Redraw the Scene tab for the GM and for players so that it does those two things. **The mechanics stay:** the commands, what is logged, Undo and its refusals, what is hidden from whom. You change arrangement, hierarchy, words on controls, and how a procedure is presented.

## The organising idea (the lead's reading of his feedback; say if the mockups argue against it)
A standing screen that stays calm and shows where things stand, and **short guided sequences of pop-up sheets** for the things that are procedures: starting a session or an expedition, moving, an activity, an encounter check, making camp, the night. His words: "It should pop up a sheet with option by option"; "Making camp should kick off a process of pop-up sheets navigating the GM and players through the activity"; "the encounter checks should be pop-up sheets: call to action and attention"; "selecting should close the sheet and move to the next step"; on camp as built: "long and very difficult to digest or understand step by step".

## What he asked for, piece by piece
1. **The start card** (GM, between expeditions). "Start session should be a prominent button." "Do I start expedition or start session?" A sheet that asks option by option, different for: the first time in a campaign; the first time after downtime; the start of each session (list any other occasion you find in 05). The three modes (dungeon, wilderness travel, settlement) as a toggle between three. Say what date and start time to set. **Where?** opens a sheet to pick from a hierarchical list (the region, its areas, its places), not a text box. (Today a typed name links to nothing; the place picker is an open pull request, #532: read its plan, don't design against it.)
2. **The expedition's title card.** Improve it. "X days till rest."
3. **The place panel** (GM). "Where now?" becomes **"Move on"**, apart from Hide all, and prominent. Notes start expanded and are filled by the pack with what the pieces don't cover. The shown/hidden eye: "so much better and more characterful" (keep a second cue beside the picture). The + Piece form is "ugly". For a settlement, take as the model the arrangement, formatting and cross-linking of the settlement pages in the fae-woodland setting's book for the GM (the Pack Creator is writing a private description of what those pages do: ask the lead for it; structure only, never the book's text or names).
4. **Marching order.** "Circles with the character portrait in the centre and glowing if holding a torch... that was the very first concept we had for this app." Prettier; clear when a tile is ready to drag (colour change or glow). Work out: a second cue with the glow (never colour alone), different light sources, no portrait yet (initials), ranks (who walks beside whom), statuses on a circle (Exhausted showed tonight as a letter badge), the locked state, the split party.
5. **Under Travel points** (GM: on foot, riding, cart; Move…, Spend the day looking, Set by hand…, Mark lost). "Unclear of purpose and seems ill-organized. I need information buttons explaining rules and behaviour." The breakdown sheet "reads like a wall of text".
6. **Move the party.** "A complete design overhaul"; "the bottom section needs to be more easily read and recognised quickly". In a defined region (point to point, or written hexes) the sheet shows only the options possible from where the party is, and where each leads ("Unknown" if not yet explored or revealed). This is also the shape his December campaign needs (an adventure with linked spots moved between by turns): design it so both fit.
7. **Tables.** "Tables loses the chevron and becomes a button that pops up a sheet. The actual pinned tables should be rows where 'Encounter tables' reads."
8. **The step icons** (the row of small round icons). On hover, the step's name and a glow; "make the icons pop more".
9. **The ⋯ menu's actions** (Make camp, Encounter check, Travelling in darkness, Advance by…, End expedition, End session). "Do we not have room for these to be buttons on the screen? I like the icons." The lead's suggestion: the daily ones on the screen with their icons; End expedition and End session stay in the menu. Those icons are the reference for the tab's icon style.
10. **Weather.** The check selects the row in the table with a tap, not a typed number. On the player's side today the result folds into a small icon by the date and the player gets a number box with no table.
11. **The GM's answer to a secret activity.** "Functionality works, but it's ugly. Ability to search or pick what the note says from a list. I'm going to usually describe it narratively and reveal it from the Scene tab via tap." So revealing a piece is one tap from the answer. (A missing control: nowhere to set the cost the player was told the GM sets; the Builder is fixing the function; leave room for it.)
12. **The player's Scene screen.** His words on it were blunt: a design overhaul, "propose mockups for all". Seen: activity buttons of different sizes, one wrapping; no heading for them (the gate list expects "Around you"); an information icon floating alone; marching tiles with heavy borders and a stray dash; a Rest button alone; the weather behind an icon; the Camp card far below the fold; nothing announcing a shown piece, a new day, or camp being made.
13. **Camp**, as a guided sequence for the GM and for players: who sees which step, what the others see meanwhile, stepping back or skipping, the GM following the whole at a glance. Today: nine sections in one card, each under a paragraph of the pack's rule text. The rule belongs one tap away, formatted (see the readability scan, F53 in the rewalk notes: sub-headings run into sentences, page references).
14. **The Rest day card** must not be missable: tonight the whole party became Exhausted because nobody answered it before Next day.
