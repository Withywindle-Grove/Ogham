# Paper test: magic in play (set up 2026-11-05; not yet walked)

A paper dry run (the `design-session` skill) before magic in play (08, M5c; `plans/magic-in-play.md`) is built. This file holds the setup and the planned steps; Francis's answers are recorded under each step as the run is walked, and the rulings follow it. Aliases and invented names only; the packs' own rules stay in `packs/` and in a private survey note (the pack session's summaries, with page references).

## 1. Setup

**Scenario and goal.** One day of an expedition, walked twice, once per setting: the casters prepare at dawn, cast while exploring, cast in a fight, turn the dead, conjure a creature, find a scroll and a spell, then rest and get their magic back. The run must reach: every kind of magic used once, spent and recovered, with the fewest taps and nothing hidden shown.

**Packs.** classic-advanced and fae-woodland (the newest in `packs/_current`), and daemonology for one step (commanding).

**Topics the two settings handle differently** (the run touches each; topics only, the rules are in the one-pagers):

| Topic | classic-advanced | fae-woodland |
|---|---|---|
| What a spell book holds | a limit tied to what the caster can memorise; an optional rule lifts it | a fixed number per book; several books |
| Preparing | study or prayer after a night's rest; the same spell twice when the level has room | the same; a shrine can add one extra blessing |
| Reversed forms | chosen at memorising for book casters, at casting for prayer casters | none printed |
| Casting in a fight | needs voice and hands; a caster hurt before acting loses the spell | declared before initiative; hurt before acting loses it |
| Turning | a table by the turner's level and the creature's kind, then a roll for how many | one roll read in bands, modified by the level gap, once per turn |
| Magic without slots | a kindred's mental powers (uses per day) | glamours (no cost), runes (uses by day, week or once), knacks (some once a day) |
| Late or small casters | several classes gain a few spells at higher levels | — |

**Characters (invented).**
- *Run A, classic-advanced:* **Wren**, a book caster, level 3; **Brother Alder**, a prayer caster, level 3; **Pike**, a thief who finds the scroll; a stone-kin with a mental power.
- *Run B, fae-woodland:* **Tamsin**, a book caster with one lesser rune; **Sister Moss**, a friar; **Ilse**, an elf with glamours; **Burr**, a small woodland kindred with a knack.
- The GM; a tablet for the GM, phones for the players; a shared table view in run B.

**Screens.** The Player screen's Spells and Abilities sections (built); the full sheet's Magic section (sheet order 3, #482); 05 Spells as ruled (M7-3), with no mockup yet for Prepare or the cast sheet: both are described in words during the run and drawn after it.

**What it tests.** Built: prepared spells listed, ability dots, the Magic section (reading only). Being designed: a character's magic as stored state, Cast with Undo, Prepare, getting magic back, the other kinds of magic, learning in play, scrolls, turning and commanding, conjured creatures. **Provisional going in:** turning's shape (a5-2) and commanding (ao-3); whether casters get their own tab (sr-4); a spell book as an item that can be lost (◈).

## 3. The walk

Walked with Francis from 2026-11-05. Mockups for this run are rough drafts, light only (Francis).

### Step 1 — Dawn: Wren prepares (run A, a book caster)
- **At the table:** the party wakes in camp; Wren's player studies her book: Mire lamp twice, as yesterday, and Reed whisper.
- **The app shows:** on her phone, Spells › Prepare ›: "Same as yesterday" with yesterday's choices under it; her book by level ("Level 1 · 2 a day · 2 chosen"), each spell with its one-line blurb, duration and range and a − N + stepper; a reversed form as a two-way choice under the spell (her class picks at memorising); a name opens the rules. Others see nothing until she has chosen. Mockup: `../mockups/magic/01-prepare.html`.
- **Taps:** Same as yesterday 2; a fresh choice of three about 5; no Done (changes save as made).
- **Francis:** keep the stepper. Prepare is open at any time, but what is chosen only becomes ready (castable) after the next rest, or as the setting's rules say; keep the prompt on the GM's rest. A full level explaining itself in a pop-up is right.
- ⚑ 1 The Prepare rows use the − N + stepper (replaces 05's "tap a number to add one, hold to clear").
- ⚑ 2 Prepare is open to the player at any time; the choice becomes ready at the next rest (or by the setting's rule), not at once. The GM's rest prompt stays ("Prepare spells ›" on each caster's screen).
- ⚑ 3 A full level: + opens a pop-up ("Level 1 is full · take one out first"), never a swap and never a disabled button.

### Step 2 — Dawn: Brother Alder prays (run A, a prayer caster)
- **At the table:** the GM taps the night's rest; Alder's player wants the usual: Mending hands twice, Candle ward.
- **The app shows:** the GM's rest prompt once ("Casters get their spells back · Wren, Brother Alder"); on Alder's phone a quiet "Prepare spells ›"; Prepare lists every spell of the levels he can cast, chosen first, the rest folded ("8 more ›").
- **Francis asks:** what is the sequence, and how does a party leave a rest today? **Answer:** in the wilderness the GM taps Make camp, the Camp card runs its steps, and **Next day** applies the whole camp and starts the new day (no separate "break camp"); in town the clock goes night to morning; in a dungeon the night's rest is the GM's Full rest. Nothing marks "still in camp, in the morning".
- **Francis:** yesterday carries over, but the caster gets the prompt and a chance to prepare first. A caster chooses up until the party leaves the rest. The first move or time step after the morning is the right closing moment (no Break camp tap). Chosen first for the long list. An empty slot after the party has left stays empty for the player; the GM can fix it.
- ⚑ 4 **The morning window.** After the rest is applied, each rested caster's screen prompts "Your spells are back · Same as yesterday · Choose…"; the GM's screen names who hasn't answered ("Waiting: Alder"), blocking nothing. The window closes at the group's first move or time step after the rest (wilderness, town and dungeon alike).
- ⚑ 5 **Carry-over.** When the window closes, yesterday's set becomes ready for anyone who hasn't answered, logged ("Alder: same as yesterday") with Undo. The app never picks a spell: with no yesterday (a first morning, a new slot) those slots stay empty and the Waiting line counts them ("Tamsin · 2 to choose"); with fewer slots than yesterday the set carries as far as it fits, in the order chosen, and the log says what dropped.
- ⚑ 6 **After the window: strict for the player.** A change of mind, or an empty slot, waits for the next rest. The GM can fill or change anyone's spells at any time (logged, Undo).
- ⚑ 7 A prayer caster's list shows what is chosen first, then the rest folded.
- Mockup for the next steps: `../mockups/magic/02-cast.html` (the Spells section, one-tap casting, the cast sheet).

### Step 3 — Exploring: Wren casts a light (run A)
- **At the table:** the last torch is guttering in a flooded corridor; Wren casts Mire lamp.
- **The app shows:** Cast on her phone: the spell is crossed out, says "cast", drops to the foot of its level; a toast "Mire lamp cast · the light is lit · Undo"; the light joins the party's lights with its duration on the time track; a duration rolled at the table reads "? turns · Set" on the GM's console; the log has the cast.
- **Taps:** Wren 2; the GM 0, or 2 to enter a rolled duration.
- **Francis:** each spell works differently and some have several options: keep the target narrative, and track the duration. Undo from the log for both the player and the GM, with the usual restrictions. Yes to a timer for any spell with a duration.
- ⚑ 8 **A spell's target stays narrative.** Cast never asks who or where; the app tracks the cast, its duration, and what the spell brings by itself (a light, a condition, a timer). (Changes the mockup's "Who?" sheet: withdrawn.)
- ⚑ 9 A cast can be undone from the log by the player or the GM, under the log's usual rules, after the toast has gone.
- ⚑ 10 Every spell with a duration puts a quiet timer on the track, even when the app tracks nothing else about it, so the GM is reminded when it ends.

### Step 4 — A spell with a real choice (run A)

- **The app shows:** Alder's Cast opens one small sheet, "Which form?", then Cast; the log reads "Brother Alder cast Break lantern (Mend lantern, reversed)". Wren's Cast is one tap: her prepared spell already reads its form.
- **Taps:** Alder 3; Wren 1.
- **Francis:** the sheet opening only for the form and a conjured count is correct for now (it may change by spell). A spell's light showing as the caster's, the GM able to move it: fine for now.
- **Francis asks:** for heals and damage, who normally modifies hit points by our rules? **Answer:** by the fight screens review (2026-11-01) the GM enters all damage and healing, players never, "for now, revisit after M6's gate"; today's build still has the player's own − and + from before that.
- **Francis (corrects):** it is fine for players to control their own hit points. The player who casts rolls the healing, and the target changes their own HP. It also lets the GM say "you were all hit for 10" and have each player change theirs, saving the GM time. The reminder is in words; no GM shortcut for now.
- ⚑ 11 The cast sheet opens only for a choice the app records: the form, for a class that chooses it at casting, and the number a conjuring spell brings. For now; a spell may add to the list.
- ⚑ 12 A spell's light shows as the caster's; the GM can move it like any light. For now.
- ⚑ 13 **Players change their own character's hit points, damage and healing alike;** the GM can change anyone's. (Supersedes the fight screens review's "party damage is entered by the GM; the Player screen has no Take damage"; it returns to the fight dry run's ruling 6. - ⚑ 14 After a cast that calls for a roll or a target, the caster's screen shows a reminder in words ("Roll it at the table and name your target"); the player enters nothing, and the GM gets no shortcut.

### Step 5 — A fight starts: the caster is hurt before casting (run A; the rule is the same in run B)
- **At the table:** bog lurkers burst from the water; Wren means to cast Reed whisper, the lurkers act first and one claws her. A caster hurt before acting loses the spell, and it counts as cast.
- **Proposed:** a quiet "Spell lost?" line on the hit-point pad listing her ready spells, one tap marking it "lost" (05's ruling, moved to the caster's own pad).
- **Francis (corrects):** make "spell lost" a reminder toast. A player who must mark a spell lost taps Cast on it. The GM sees the toast too. Shown whenever the setting's rule is on, not only in a fight. For a lost spell that would start a light or a timer: leave it for now (the player or GM ends it by hand); "I can't think of a better way to handle it that doesn't take away from play. Maybe a 'no effect' or 'fizzle?' toast that the player can tap to block effects."
- ⚑ 15 **"Spell lost" is a reminder toast**, on the caster's screen and the GM's, when a caster with ready spells takes damage and the setting has the rule ("Wren was hurt · a spell she was casting is lost"). No tappable list and no separate "lost" state: the spell is marked by tapping Cast. Declaring before initiative stays at the table. (Replaces 05's "Spell lost?" line on the damage pad and its Lost mark.)
- ⚑ 16 Casting a lost spell does what any cast does; what it wrongly starts (a light, a timer) is ended by hand. For now.
- **Parking lot (try at the table test):** a "No effect" tap on the cast toast, which keeps the spell spent and blocks what it would start.

### Step 6 — Turning the dead, the table style (run A)
- **At the table:** three drowned dead rise from the reeds; Brother Alder turns them. The turner's level and the creature's kind give no effect, a number to roll, turned or destroyed; a second roll says how much of the group, weakest first.
- **Offered:** option 1, a reference the player reads (the trait opens the rule and the table with the turner's row picked out; nothing entered); option 2, a prompt that records kind, outcome and how many (about 5 taps, leaning on M6).
- **Francis:** option 1, but can the app help with the target numbers from the encounter? A log line, yes. Track the use.
- **Francis asks:** can it help with target numbers? **Answer:** yes once an encounter's foes exist (M6), if the pack marks each such creature with its column of the table; but the number gives away what the creature is, so it follows the existing campaign setting "Monster AC and saves" (learned in play · always shown · never shown): the GM always sees it, players per that setting.
- **Francis:** yes to both suggestions.
- ⚑ 17 **Turning is a reference, not a form.** The trait opens the rule in a few words and the pack's table with the turner's own row picked out; the second roll is described in words; outcomes and which creatures flee stay at the table.
- ⚑ 18 **Help from the encounter.** When an encounter has creatures the table covers, the GM's console shows the number needed for each ("Turning · Alder: Drowned dead 7+ · Barrow lord, no effect"); players see it on their own table as the "Monster AC and saves" setting allows. Needs each such stat block to name its column (pack work) and M6's encounters.
- ⚑ 19 A **Turn** button at the foot of the reference logs the attempt ("Brother Alder tried to turn the dead"), with optional outcome chips that vanish if ignored, as an encounter's Talk and Avoid details do.
- ⚑ 20 Where the rules or the campaign limit attempts, Turn spends a use (a pip) that comes back when the setting says (per encounter, per turn).

### Step 7 — Turning by one roll, and commanding (run B, then daemonology)

- **Francis:** yes to all three.
- ⚑ 21 **One turning shape for every book** (settles a5-2 and ao-3): the class's turning ability points at a table (laid out by turner level and creature kind, or by roll bands); the pack supplies the outcome words used on the log's chips; an optional limit on attempts and when it comes back; a note line for special cases.
- ⚑ 22 A special case such as "counts as tougher unless it serves the turner's own lord" is a note the GM reads; the app matches nothing.
